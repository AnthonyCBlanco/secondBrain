## Volume by Disks and Washers

### Idea
The method of disks and washers calculates the volume of a 3-dimensional solid of revolution generated by rotating a 2-dimensional planar region about a line called the axis of revolution.

In this method, the solid is sliced into thin circular slices **perpendicular** to the axis of revolution:
- **Disks:** When the cross-section between the curve and the axis is solid without internal cavities, each slice is a circular disk with area $A = \pi R^2$.
- **Washers:** When the region being revolved does not touch the axis of revolution throughout its domain, the cross-section contains a circular hole, forming a washer (annulus) with outer radius $R$ and inner radius $r$, having area $A = \pi(R^2 - r^2)$.

Integrating the cross-sectional area multiplied by the infinitesimal thickness ($dx$ or $dy$) yields the total volume.

### Formally
#### General Slicing Principle
$$V = \int_a^b A(x) \, dx \quad \text{or} \quad V = \int_c^d A(y) \, dy$$

#### The Disk Method (Solid Cross-Sections)
- **Rotation about a Horizontal Axis (e.g., $x$-axis or line $y = k$):**
  $$V = \pi \int_a^b [R(x)]^2 \, dx$$
  where $R(x)$ is the perpendicular distance from the boundary curve to the axis of rotation.
- **Rotation about a Vertical Axis (e.g., $y$-axis or line $x = h$):**
  $$V = \pi \int_c^d [R(y)]^2 \, dy$$
  where $R(y)$ is the perpendicular distance from the boundary curve to the axis of rotation.

#### The Washer Method (Hollow Cross-Sections)
- **Rotation about a Horizontal Axis:**
  $$V = \pi \int_a^b \left( [R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2 \right) dx$$
  where $R(x)$ is the outer radius and $r(x)$ is the inner radius.
- **Rotation about a Vertical Axis:**
  $$V = \pi \int_c^d \left( [R(y)]^2 - [r(y)]^2 \right) dy$$
  where $R(y)$ is the outer radius and $r(y)$ is the inner radius.

*(Note: The integrand represents the difference of squared radii $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$, not the square of the difference $\pi(R - r)^2$.)*

### Example
Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, the $x$-axis ($y = 0$), and the line $x = 4$ about the $x$-axis.

**Step 1: Identify the axis of revolution and boundaries**
- Axis of revolution: $x$-axis ($y = 0$).
- Bounds along the axis: $x \in [0, 4]$.

**Step 2: Determine the cross-sectional geometry**
Slices perpendicular to the $x$-axis form solid circular disks with radius:
$$R(x) = \sqrt{x} - 0 = \sqrt{x}$$

**Step 3: Set up the volume integral**
$$V = \pi \int_0^4 [R(x)]^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx$$

**Step 4: Evaluate the integral**
$$V = \pi \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^4 = \pi \left( \frac{4^2}{2} - 0 \right) = \pi \left( \frac{16}{2} \right) = 8\pi$$

**Step 5: Conclusion**
The exact volume of the solid of revolution is $8\pi$ cubic units.
