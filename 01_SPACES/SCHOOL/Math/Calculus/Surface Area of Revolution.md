## Surface Area of Revolution

### Idea
The surface area of revolution measures the outer lateral surface area of a 3-dimensional solid generated by revolving a continuous 2-dimensional curve about an axis of rotation.

Approximating the revolving curve by microscopic line segments of arc length $dL$ generates thin geometric bands called frustums of cones. The surface area of each differential band is given by $dS = 2\pi r \cdot dL$, where $r$ is the radius of revolution (the perpendicular distance from the curve to the axis) and $dL$ is the differential arc length along the curve. 

Integrating these circular bands along the interval yields the complete lateral surface area.

### Formally
#### General Differential Surface Area Element
$$dS = 2\pi r \, dL$$

#### Rotation About the $x$-Axis ($y = 0$)
Here the radius of revolution is $r = y$:
- **Integrating with respect to $x$ ($y = f(x)$):**
  $$S = 2\pi \int_a^b f(x) \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$
- **Integrating with respect to $y$ ($x = g(y)$):**
  $$S = 2\pi \int_c^d y \sqrt{1 + [g'(y)]^2} \, dy$$

#### Rotation About the $y$-Axis ($x = 0$)
Here the radius of revolution is $r = x$:
- **Integrating with respect to $x$ ($y = f(x)$):**
  $$S = 2\pi \int_a^b x \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2} \, dx$$
- **Integrating with respect to $y$ ($x = g(y)$):**
  $$S = 2\pi \int_c^d g(y) \sqrt{1 + [g'(y)]^2} \, dy$$

#### Parametric Curves $(x(t), y(t))$ on $[\alpha, \beta]$
- **About the $x$-axis:**
  $$S_x = 2\pi \int_\alpha^\beta y(t) \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$$
- **About the $y$-axis:**
  $$S_y = 2\pi \int_\alpha^\beta x(t) \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$$

### Example
Find the area of the surface generated by revolving the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ about the $x$-axis.

**Step 1: Compute the derivative and simplify the arc length element $dL$**
$$y = \sqrt{x} \implies y' = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \implies (y')^2 = \frac{1}{4x}$$
$$dL = \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4x}} \, dx = \sqrt{\frac{4x + 1}{4x}} \, dx = \frac{\sqrt{4x + 1}}{2\sqrt{x}} \, dx$$

**Step 2: Set up the surface area integral for rotation about the $x$-axis ($r = y = \sqrt{x}$)**
$$S = 2\pi \int_0^2 y \, dL = 2\pi \int_0^2 \sqrt{x} \left( \frac{\sqrt{4x + 1}}{2\sqrt{x}} \right) dx = \pi \int_0^2 \sqrt{4x + 1} \, dx$$

**Step 3: Evaluate using $u$-substitution**
Let $u = 4x + 1 \implies du = 4 \, dx \implies dx = \frac{1}{4}du$.
- Lower limit: $x = 0 \implies u = 4(0) + 1 = 1$
- Upper limit: $x = 2 \implies u = 4(2) + 1 = 9$

$$S = \pi \int_1^9 u^{1/2} \left(\frac{1}{4}du\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} \left[ \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \right]_1^9 = \frac{\pi}{6} (9^{3/2} - 1^{3/2})$$
$$= \frac{\pi}{6}(27 - 1) = \frac{26\pi}{6} = \frac{13\pi}{3}$$

**Step 4: Conclusion**
The total surface area of revolution is $\frac{13\pi}{3}$ square units.
